The why

What isn't captured disappears.

I was the kid who took apart the Nintendo to see how it worked. I never really stopped — I just moved on to bigger machines.

I lost my mom at 13. Last year I lost my grandmother's stories, which is a different kind of loss: she was still here, and the stories weren't written down anywhere. That's the whole reason StoryVault exists. Parent Playbook exists because my six-year-old started tackle football and I had no idea what I was doing. AllFantasy exists because I was tired of four tabs.

I'm not a traditional coder. I'm a product architect who works AI-native — I diagnose the real problem, design the fix, and direct the build. It means we ship in weeks what used to take a quarter, and we show the work while we do it. Ugly first versions included.

How we actually work

The process

Take it apart. Then build it back.

01

The teardown

One call, free. You walk us through what's broken and we ask the annoying questions until the actual problem shows up — which is often not the one you called about.

02

The plan

A written scope with a fixed price and a real date. What's in, what's out, what it costs. No hourly meter, no surprise invoice at the end.

03

The build

You get a working link in week one, not a status update. Progress is something you click, not something you read about.

04

The handoff

You own the code, the repo and the accounts. We'll keep running it if you want us to, but you're never locked in. That's on purpose.

Tell us what's broken

If it annoys you every week, it's worth building.

Bring us the workaround you've been living with. First call is free and we'll tell you straight if we're the wrong shop for it.
